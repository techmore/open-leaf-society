## Is this legal?
Open Leaf itself sells nothing. It collects data, writes standards and runs a community fund. Member shops agree to sell only products that are legal under federal and Pennsylvania law, confirmed by lab test. Those rules tighten on **December 11, 2026**, and many products on shelves today won't qualify after that.

## Is this about marijuana?
Today, it's about hemp. Hemp and marijuana are the same species; the legal line is THC content. We care about the whole plant as a resource: medicine, fiber, food and raw material. The shops we work with sell hemp and CBD. Medical marijuana is run by the state program, and adult-use marijuana isn't legal in Pennsylvania. Open Leaf follows the law. Any policy advocacy is done by the member association, never the foundation. More on [the plant]({{ '/plant/' | relative_url }}).

## Does the township have to pay for or pass anything?
No. Reports are free. Phase 0 is only conversations.

## Can our township tax hemp sales?
Not directly today. Pennsylvania townships can only levy taxes the state authorizes, and there's no local hemp or cannabis sales tax. What townships can use right now: real local data for planning, voluntary community agreements with member shops (never tied to permits or zoning), and the 1% Community Dividend, which reaches residents directly.

## Who sees my purchase data?
Open Leaf never receives your individual purchases. Your shop and its payment processor keep normal sales records, like any store. Only monthly totals reach Open Leaf's data steward, small groups are hidden before anything is published, and no customer identifiers are collected. See [Data]({{ '/data/' | relative_url }}).

## Why ISO 9001?
It's the world's most widely used quality-management standard, and the founders have used it. Our standard borrows its structure (written procedures, records, internal audits, corrective action) so results are **reproducible**: it's written so different auditors checking the same evidence reach the same result. The Open Leaf Standard itself is open and free; ISO's own text is not.

## Is the 1% fund a lottery?
No. Applying is free, there's no purchase and no entry fee, and recipients have to meet published need-based criteria. A public random draw only sets the order when there are more eligible applicants than funds. Counsel will review the program before the first grant.

## What's an RFC?
A "Request for Comments": a draft published so anyone can suggest changes before it's final. Our [charter]({{ '/rfc/0001/' | relative_url }}) is RFC-0001. Comment on [GitHub]({{ site.repo }}/issues) or at a meetup.

## Is Open Leaf a nonprofit?
It's forming as one. It isn't incorporated or tax-exempt yet, so contributions aren't tax-deductible yet.

## Does Open Leaf sell products or take a cut of sales?
No. Member dues fund the work. The 1% Community Dividend is held by the Open Leaf foundation in a separate, restricted account and used only for grants. It never pays for operations.

## Do shops have to use Rupert?
No. The data format is open, so any point-of-sale system can send it, and pilot shops can use a spreadsheet. Rupert is the free, open-source reference version.
